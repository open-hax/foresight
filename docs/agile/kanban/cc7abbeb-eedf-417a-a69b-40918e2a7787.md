# Replace the Promethean FSM with an agent-first board workflow

Promethean has 14 states and 15 transition rules. Thirteen of those rules are
`:always-allow`. One checks a WIP limit (`todo → in_progress`) and one runs a
build gate (`in_progress → review`).

That ratio is the whole finding. The states were doing their work *on the
author* — each move was a place to stop and reconsider what you were committing
to, which is real value for one person holding a whole system in their head. But
that value lives in the pause, and an agent does not pause. For an agent,
twelve of those distinctions carry no machine-checkable meaning, so walking
`incoming → accepted → breakdown → ready → todo → in_progress` is six writes
that assert nothing and can fail to be true the moment they are made.

The author's own account of why the board grew so many states is the same
finding from the inside, and it is worth keeping verbatim rather than
paraphrasing into a rationale:

> When I used the rejected column, when that was my personal board, I wrote
> every card, and I read every card. So by the time it reached that state, it
> was a judgement call.

That is the load-bearing condition. `rejected` was a trustworthy state because
one person had read everything and was the judge. Nothing in the column enforced
that; the author did, by being the only writer. The states were a private
protocol between someone and themselves, and they worked. They stop working the
moment the board has writers who did not read every card — which is now.

So this is not a claim that Promethean was badly designed. It is a claim that
its guarantees were carried by the author rather than by the FSM, and that they
have to be moved into the FSM or they are gone.

The replacement should not be "Promethean with fewer states." It should move the
information out of the state graph and into two things a machine can actually
check: **eligibility, derived** and **evidence, owed**.

## Why there were fourteen

Promethean was not one workflow with fourteen steps. It was several workflows
belonging to different concerns, flattened onto one axis because a board column
is the only place a board can put anything.

Product decides one set of things. Design decides another. Developers another.
Review another. Each of those is a small lifecycle owned by someone, and they
run concurrently over the same card — a card can be undecided by product and
simultaneously active for a developer. A single status enum cannot say that, so
the concerns get interleaved into one sequence and the sequence grows until it
can express the union. Fourteen states is what that costs.

**Do not mistake a state for a category.** That is the actual error, and the
notes already named it: stop looking for one noun that classifies every stage,
and let a node carry several orthogonal classifications. Base artifact, plus
declared kind, plus optional facets, plus project-local extension — validated
per asserted contract rather than by one universal schema.

The three corrections this card has already taken — claim, archived, trash
reason — were not three naming problems. They were three instances of this one
error, caught one at a time. The general form:

```
if it is reachable from everywhere        it is not a state
if it survives the transition             it is not a state
if the eligibility predicate reads it     it is not a state
if it belongs to a different concern      it is not a state
```

So the status axis stays deliberately small, and everything else becomes a
declared facet. Alpha already has the machinery — `FacetAssertion` with
`:facet/schema` and `:facet/target` in `alpha.law.markdown.schema`, composed
through the Katamorph registry. The board does not need a new mechanism for
this. It needs to stop putting other concerns' lifecycles in its status column.

## Proposed shape

States — six, each one a genuinely different answer to "who or what acts next":

```
open      nobody is working it
active    work is happening
blocked   waiting on something no agent can produce
review    work done, evidence attached, awaiting adjudication
done      accepted
trash     out of the flow
```

**One bin, not four.** Promethean's `rejected` and `archived`, plus the
`superseded` and `obsolete` distinctions that wanted to exist alongside them,
are all the same event: a decision was made that this leaves the flow. They are
different *reasons*, not different conditions, and a reason is an attribute.

```
:trash/reason  refused      considered on its merits and declined
               superseded   another card carries this now
               obsolete     the world moved; it no longer applies
               stale        sat too long; nobody is going to do it
```

`superseded` and `obsolete` carry a relation to whatever replaced or invalidated
them, so the trail survives the binning.

One of those four is not like the others: **`stale` is the only reason an agent
can derive.** Time-since-last-event is structural. The other three require
somebody to have read the card and decided. That maps exactly onto the boundary
Epiphany already drew and the root policy already states — structural automation
decides malformed, missing, and broken; it does not decide merit. So an agent
may *propose* trash-for-staleness and must not *declare* a refusal. That is the
α/μ line running through the board's own workflow.

**The claim is an attribute, not a state.** A card carries a claim — claimee
plus lease — the same way it carries points or a depends-on relation. Being
claimed is a fact *about* the card; being active is the card's condition. The
two move together most of the time, which is exactly why collapsing them is
tempting and wrong.

Two things fall out of keeping them separate, and both are gains:

- The claim survives the state. A card that reaches `review` and then `done`
  still records who did the work. Had `claimed` been a state, that identity
  would evaporate the moment the card moved on, and the provenance would have
  to be reconstructed from the ledger.
- The eligibility predicate reads the claim directly, without asking the state
  graph. "Is anyone holding this?" is an attribute lookup, not a traversal.

Every non-trivial edge carries an obligation instead of `:always-allow`:

```
open    → active    claim-gate      attaches a claim; WIP still applies
active  → open      release         detaches the claim; expiry does this too
active  → blocked   blocker-named   must name what blocks and who can clear it
blocked → open      blocker-cleared
active  → review    evidence-gate   attached evidence, not a green feeling
review  → done      acceptance      the only edge a human may be required for
review  → active    rework          findings become the next claim's input
*       → trash     reason-named    agents may only propose reason `stale`
trash   → open      recover         a bin nobody empties is just an archive
done    → open      reopen
```

The claim is attached and detached *by* those transitions, but it is never what
the transition is named after.

The states Promethean loses are not deleted, they are demoted from *state* to
*attribute* — the same demotion the claim gets, applied to the rest of the
graph. Readiness then becomes a **pure predicate** over the card plus the
ledger, rather than a column someone has to move a card into:

```
eligible? = has acceptance criteria
          ∧ is sized
          ∧ every depends-on relation is done
          ∧ no unmet human decision
          ∧ not leased by another agent
```

`icebox`, `incoming`, `accepted`, `breakdown`, `ready`, and `todo` all collapse
into `open` plus that predicate. An agent asks "what may I pick up?" and gets a
computed answer instead of trusting that six humans-shaped writes happened in
the right order. `testing` and `document` collapse into the evidence obligation
on `active → review`, which is where they were already being enforced.

`archived` collapses too, and not into `trash`. It is reachable from all
thirteen other Promethean states, including `done` and `rejected`, which is the
tell: a state reachable from everywhere is not a state. Archiving answers
"should this still be on my screen" — a retention fact, not a condition of the
work. So it becomes an attribute that composes with any status. A `done` card
can be archived. A `trash` card can sit visible for a while first.

## Why this is architecture and not board hygiene

A card is an Artifact. A transition is an Event. A gate is a Reaction condition.
The ledger under `.ημ/kanban-events/` is Clio. Building this FSM *is* the first
real instance of the Alpha/Rheos/Clio model on a corpus we already maintain and
already feel the pain of — which is a far more honest test than a fake graph.

## Acceptance

- The FSM is portable `.cljc` data, not a CLJS map inside a Node package.
  `rheos.backend.law.fsm` is currently `.cljs` and requires `node:child_process`
  in the same namespace as the pure transition table; the decision function and
  the gate runner separate.
- `evaluate-transition` stays pure and total. Side-effecting gates run only
  after structural admission — Promethean already gets this right; keep it.
- Eligibility is a pure predicate with its own tests, and disagreeing with it is
  a finding rather than an override.
- The claim is modelled as an attribute with its own shape — claimee, lease,
  acquired-at — and is readable independently of status.
- The lease is real: a claim that is never released expires, detaches, and
  returns the card to `open`. Agents crash.
- A claim on a `done` or `trash` card is retained as provenance, not cleared.
- `:trash/reason` is a required, structured value. Prose is not a reason.
- `superseded` and `obsolete` require a relation to the card or fact that
  displaced them; a bin entry with no trail is refused.
- An agent may set `:trash/reason :stale` and may not set the other three. The
  refusal is structural, not a convention agents are asked to honour.
- `archived` is an attribute orthogonal to status, settable on any card,
  including `done`. It is not a terminal state and nothing transitions into it.
- Recovery from `trash` works and preserves the original reason in the ledger,
  so a card binned as `stale` and later recovered shows both facts.
- Evidence is structured and refers to receipts, not prose. "Tests pass" is a
  claim; a receipt naming the suite, the counts, and the runtime is evidence.
- A migration maps every existing Promethean status onto the new shape, and the
  four cards already on this board survive it without losing history.
- Promethean remains available. This is a second FSM, selected by
  `openhax.kanban.edn`, not a deletion of the one that works for a human
  working alone.

## Still open — deliberately

**Two states are the next candidates for the same error, and are not resolved
here.** Applying the test above rather than guessing:

- `blocked` fails "the eligibility predicate reads it." The predicate already
  contains `every depends-on relation is done`. If a blocker is a relation, then
  blocked is derived, exactly as `ready` was, and the state is redundant with
  the thing computing it.
- `review` fails "it belongs to a different concern." Review is the reviewer's
  lifecycle, not the card's. A card in review has finished *developer* work; the
  status column is reporting someone else's queue.

Both may still earn their place — a derived condition can be worth naming, and
review is where the only human-required edge lives. But that argument has to be
made, not assumed, and the last three times it was assumed it was wrong.

**The name is parked.** Naming it for this project is circular: Prometheus means
forethought, so Foresight *is* Promethean, translated. Let it sit. Names here
have arrived late and been revised more than once, and this one has no reason to
be different.

Gate names and the eligibility predicate are proposals, not decisions. The
finding that stands on its own is the 13-of-15 `:always-allow` ratio in
`eta-mu/packages/rheos/src/rheos/backend/law/fsm.cljs`.

Revision 1: `claimed` was corrected to `active` by the author. Claiming is an
attribute relating the card to its claimee, not a state — the card's condition
and the bookkeeping about who holds it are different facts. The earlier draft
named it as a state while the eligibility predicate already read it as an
attribute, which was an inconsistency in the proposal rather than a naming
preference. The six states now describe conditions only.

Revision 2: `dropped` became `trash`, and the author supplied the reasoning.
`rejected`, `dropped`, `superseded`, and `obsolete` are "different words for
different kinds of trash cans" — different ways a card leaves the flow, not
different conditions once it has left. One bin, four reasons. `stale` was the
author's addition and turned out to be the only reason an agent can derive,
which is what produced the propose-versus-declare split above.

The word `trash` is the author's and is deliberately plain. It is the token most
easily changed later; the one-bin-plus-reason structure is the part that matters
and should survive any rename.
