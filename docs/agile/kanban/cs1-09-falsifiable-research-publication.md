# CS1.09 — Make the cognitive-substrate hypothesis publishably falsifiable

## User story

**As a** researcher/writer,\
**I want** each major substrate claim expressed as a reproducible hypothesis,
experiment, countermodel, and retained result,\
**so that** the work can produce an honest blog post or series whether the idea
works, partially works, or turns out to be a beautiful but unhelpful model.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every experiment states a hypothesis, competing explanation/countermodel, measurable prediction, and falsification criterion before the result is interpreted.
- [ ] Exact code/data revisions, configuration, random seeds when applicable, and environment/tool versions are retained.
- [ ] Positive, negative, null, unstable, and failed runs remain distinguishable and reviewable.
- [ ] Charts/visualizations tell the causal story while linking back to the underlying evidence rather than replacing it.
- [ ] A result section distinguishes observation from interpretation and proposed next work.
- [ ] The public narrative can explain the math operationally to a technically capable reader without assuming formal mathematical expertise.
- [ ] Publication is not conditioned on the system producing intelligence-like emergence.

## Tasks within this story

- [ ] Choose the first two or three decisive experiments from CS1.03/08.
- [ ] Define the report schema and result bundle before running them.
- [ ] Generate a narrative artifact with diagrams/charts and explicit counterevidence.
- [ ] Record what would make the next experiment unnecessary because the hypothesis was already falsified.

## Non-goals

Marketing a successful outcome in advance, hiding dead ends, or using an LLM's
confidence as experimental evidence.
