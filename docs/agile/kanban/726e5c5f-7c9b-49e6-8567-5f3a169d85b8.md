# Walk one SME through one review, end to end, with a real caller

This is the acceptance test for the whole decomposition, and it exists because
of what the Knoxx publication epic did without it.

That epic produced twelve well-specified cards, every one implemented against
its own acceptance criteria, and a stack of green suites. Six of its namespaces
have no production caller. The plan laws, the gate, the effect boundary, the
idempotency store, the migration fold and the surface verifier are a complete
library that nothing invokes. Its end-to-end test satisfied every stated
criterion by mutating a local atom. Every `Done when` bullet in that epic was
satisfiable by a test, and none of them said *something calls this*.

So the proof for these four capabilities is not another suite. It is one real
review, performed by a person, through the composed capabilities.

> an AI walking an SME through one translation review is the real acceptance test

## The journey

```text
Repository      resolve the source artifact
     ↓
Transduction    produce a candidate + provenance
     ↓
Evaluation      open a review case, take the SME's judgment
     ↓            (accept / correct / flag / defer)
Receipt         durable fact that the judgment happened
     ↓
Representation  show the result
```

## Scope

- Drive the journey over MCP, because the EDN contracts plus the MCP surface are
  the product boundary; any web UI is a replaceable consumer of it.
- One real transduction from a real provider. Not a fixture.
- One real human judgment recorded as a real receipt.
- The SME is walked through it by an agent, since that is the interaction being
  claimed, not a form being submitted.

## Non-goals

- No dedicated review frontend. If the MCP flow cannot walk someone through a
  review, a frontend built on top of it will not fix that.
- No second domain. One review, done for real, beats two simulated.

## Done when

- A person who is not the author completes one review and the receipt exists
  afterwards, readable without the session that produced it.
- Every stage is reached through a production caller. Specifically: no stage is
  satisfied by a test double, an injected function, or an atom standing in for a
  capability that should have been invoked.
- Killing any one capability breaks the journey at that stage and says so —
  proving the stages are actually load-bearing rather than incidentally present.
- The evaluation stage runs with no `:translation/*` key reaching the generic
  model, so the same flow is reusable for an agent-output review.
- A written account of what broke the first time it was run against something
  real. Knoxx #243 found that every publication route answered 500 to every live
  request, after 980 green tests, because nothing had ever called them the way a
  real client does. Expect the equivalent here and record it.
